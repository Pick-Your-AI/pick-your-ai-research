## User decision

What will this build cost, under stated assumptions, and with what uncertainty?

In scope: unit normalisation (per token, per hour, per GB-month, per call, one-off training); scenario inputs from #2; aggregation across the active components; region, currency and conversion policy; assumptions and uncertainty ranges; how freshness and provenance are shown. Out of scope: collecting a price catalogue (the component issues and #41 own that). The prices in this record are samples that prove the units and drive the worked scenarios. They are not a catalogue.

Inputs. The Guardrails ([#35](guardrails.md)), Evaluation ([#36](evaluation.md)), Deployment ([#37](deployment.md)) and Monitoring ([#38](monitoring.md)) records sit in this folder and are cited by id: #35 rule ids (`G-R21`), #36 rule ids (`E-R14`), #37 rule and evidence ids (`D-R9`, `D-S19`) and #38 signal, threshold and rule ids (`SG56`, `TH5`, `M-R7`, `M-H14`). Rules those records own are cited here, not restated. The #2 (T0) fields that set quantities are volume (requests and tokens per period), throughput and concurrency, latency target, residency, data classification, budget (amount, currency, period) and the capability flags for T3, T4 and T5. In the actual #2 → #39 co-owner dispositions, [appletechie at `e41aa5d`](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/issues/2#issuecomment-6245701804) and [PUSHINGSQUARES at `9192d5d`](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/issues/2#issuecomment-6247690142) accept `requests`, mean/max lengths and `budgets` as inputs. Their scope retains cacheable-prefix share, request-length bands against price tiers and the peak-to-mean request rate as labelled builder-set drivers; `peak_concurrency` counts requests in flight, not a rate. At the initial 2026-10-16 capture, the other listed inputs (#5, #7, #9, #10, #14, #15, #26, #31, #40, #41) were not available in this workspace. ### Number labels

Every number carries one label. **Published** means copied from a source on the access date. **Estimated** means derived here; the formula is shown next to it and re-computed by [`cost-scenarios.py`](cost-scenarios.py) from [`cost-estimate.scenarios.json`](cost-estimate.scenarios.json). **Builder-set** means a choice the builder or user makes (for example a cache hit ratio). Counts of rows made by the builder from a cited price file or API query (909, 12, 394, 567) are **estimated**: each is the number of rows carrying one unit string, counted by the builder with the query and date given where the count appears, and anyone can recount it. No number in this record is **measured** from a running system.

### Five findings that shape the record

1. **The same workload is priced in at least nine incompatible units.** Tokens come per 1K (909 rows of the Bedrock us-east-1 price file carry the unit `1K tokens`, estimated: counted by the builder from the unit field of the AmazonBedrock offer file, version 20261016210806, recounted 2026-10-17; older Azure meters), per 1M (OpenAI, Gemini, Vertex, newer Bedrock and Azure rows) or per MTok (Anthropic) (C-P1, C-A1, C-A2). Some meters bill characters, text units, text records, Neurons, searches, PTU-hours, model-unit hours, GSUs or CMU-minutes. Some unit strings disagree with their own description: the Bedrock Reserved tier row says `1M TPM Hour` while its description says "Per Hour per 1K Output TPM" (C-A3). Normalisation therefore keeps the published unit string and a recorded factor (C-R1, C-R2).
2. **The range comes from quantities, not from price noise.** On the reference workload below, the Claude Sonnet 5.5 and gpt-6.1-sol estimates move about 4.3 times from low to high (W1: 6,700 to 29,000 USD a month; estimated, 28,600 / 6,680), the Gemini 3.1 Pro row 2.9 times and the Workers AI row 3.1 times, while every list price stays fixed. The drivers are cache hit ratio, output length, reasoning tokens and tokenizer ratio. For capacity and self-hosted modes the driver is utilisation: capacity sized for a 3-to-1 peak runs at about a third of its throughput on average (W2: 32 to 33 percent, estimated as mean load / provisioned load), and a two-replica GPU floor runs at 2.6 to 27 percent (W3, estimated the same way).
3. **Capacity costs follow the hour, not the token.** Azure provisioned throughput bills "regardless of the number of tokens consumed" (C-P2), Bedrock Provisioned Throughput on 1-month and 6-month commitments and Vertex Provisioned Throughput have terms that cannot be cancelled mid-term (C-P3, C-P4), and self-hosted GPUs bill while they run (C-S1). The only break-even statement found in the pages opened is Microsoft's: PTU and pay-as-you-go are "approximately equal at full utilization", computed for one PTU at $260.00 per month, the page's example assumption (C-P5). At the hourly Data Zone rate, gpt-5.2 PTUs never reach pay-as-you-go parity (W2: break-even utilisation 2.8, estimated). Capacity comparisons therefore show utilisation and break-even where the required inputs exist, with missing throughput or sizing retained as unknown. The capacity rows and worked capacity results also disclose **headroom not applied**: no explicit capacity-reserve input is included in their arithmetic (C-R20 and the dated #10 receiving condition below).
4. **Prices carry dates that end inside an estimate period.** Gemini 4 Argon input is "$2.00 through November 30, 2026. $4.00 starting December 1, 2026." (C-A4); OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol promotion runs "at least through November 21, 2026" (C-A1); Anthropic logs dated price changes in its release notes (C-D1). AWS says its price list files "can change anytime" (C-D2) and may raise fees on 30 days notice (AWS Customer Agreement section 3.1, C-N203); Microsoft and Google reserve the right to change prices (C-D3, C-D4). An estimate without the date of each price cannot be checked later (C-R10, C-R11).
5. **Every publisher sampled lists its prices in USD, and no list price includes tax.** Microsoft: "The currency that Microsoft uses to price all Azure services is USD" (C-A5), though Enterprise Agreements show prices in local currency (C-F10); Google: "Prices are listed in US Dollars (USD)" (C-A14), though a billing account can pay in another currency (C-A14, C-F21); OpenAI: a help-centre statement that API credit purchases are limited to USD was seen only in a search snippet (page returns HTTP 403), so it is an unchecked lead (C-F2). Taxes are excluded at AWS, Google and Azure (C-S2, C-F3, C-F4). The currency and region policy below follows from this.

Low, expected and high are a **scenario range**: each case sets every driver to the same side. They are not a statistical interval (C-R41).

**#10 quantity and headroom receiving condition (2026-10-18).** [@PUSHINGSQUARES's actual response](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/issues/10#issuecomment-6247689665), checking `9192d5d`, accepts the quantity/utilisation boundary with narrowing: each capacity line must say **headroom not applied** until an explicit input is defined. The W2 and W3 counts below use peak demand, publisher increments and a separately stated replica floor; they do not apply #10's scenario reserve. Rounding, a provider minimum and failover replicas do not establish a chosen utilisation margin. A missing reserve is not an explicit zero. No default reserve, confidence threshold or ResourceEstimate is adopted here. When that input is supplied, identify its capacity domain and basis and apply it once before deriving the quantity to price; a memory reserve does not supply a throughput margin. The `h` already used in W1/W2 is the cache-hit ratio, not the headroom fraction in [#10's memory method](../T2-infrastructure/gpu-compute.md#headroom-and-measurement-handoff-disposition-2026-10-18). Likewise, #10's proposed `peak_concurrency` is requests in flight, not requests per second or the peak-to-mean request ratio. Neither conversion can be inferred without its own workload and timing evidence.

### Integration constraints from the unit-normalisation draft

Source check 2026-10-18: [NIST binary prefixes](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Units/binary.html) defines decimal GB as 10^9 bytes and GiB as 2^30 bytes. [EBS pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ebs/pricing/) explicitly uses GB = 1024^3 bytes; gp3 storage/IOPS/throughput bill per second with a 60-second minimum, and its worked example uses a 30-day month. Retain that source unit when deriving a price per decimal GB: the exact factor is 10^9 / 2^30 = 0.931322574615478515625. A vendor label with no byte definition remains unknown. These additions refine U21/C-R32 without changing frozen v1.

For a selected `period_days`, hourly usage is `24 * period_days`; 30 days is 720 hours. The 730-hour convention in W1–W4 is a separate explicitly chosen period, not an uncertainty bound for a fixed 30-day estimate. Instance counts are integral, but duration may be fractional: one eight-GPU instance for 0.5 billable hours consumes four GPU-hours. Billing increments and minima apply before pricing, per the vendor's actual scope. Allowances apply once per scope, not once per component. The [#113 reconciliation](sibling-reconciliation.md#113-cost-normalisation) retains the unresolved rounding/representation and contract questions; none is silently settled by the calculator's illustrative floating-point arithmetic.

## Capacity vs usage

Capacity lines (PTU, model unit, Reserved tier TPM, GSU, node, GPU) bill for time held. Usage lines bill for tokens, calls or bytes. The comparison method:

1. **Size the capacity** from the T0 peak: units = ceil(peak load in the publisher's unit / throughput per unit, to the increment), at least the minimum. The throughput per unit must come from the publisher (Azure input TPM per PTU, Vertex tokens per second per GSU, a published engine benchmark). Bedrock does not publish model-unit throughput (C-P3), so a Bedrock PT estimate needs the account team's figure.
2. **Cost the period**: units × unit price × hours, or term totals for reservations, plus overflow tokens at the usage price where the publisher spills over (Azure opt-in, Vertex by default, Bedrock Reserved to Standard; Databricks rate-limits instead).
3. **Utilisation** u = mean load / (units × throughput per unit).
4. **Break-even utilisation** u* = capacity price per hour / (usage price per token × tokens a unit serves per hour). Below u*, usage pricing is cheaper. The FinOps Foundation states the same point: cost per token purchased is lower on provisioned capacity, but "the cost per tokens processed could be higher depending on your utilization" (C-U12).
5. **Show both** next to each other with u and u*, and the commitment term, minimum and cancellation terms beside the capacity figure (C-R20). Show **headroom not applied** when the capacity reserve is unspecified; retain missing throughput, utilisation or break-even inputs as unknown. A complete price does not resolve an unsized capacity quantity.

## Uncertainty presentation (proposal)

1. **Three figures, one basis.** Every line and every total shows low, expected and high, and names its basis: "scenario range" when the cases set drivers to one side (this record), "80% interval" only when a method yields one, as AWS Cost Explorer's forecast does ("a prediction interval of 80%", C-U13; the GetCostForecast API accepts 51 to 99, AWS Cost Management API Reference, C-N202). A scenario range is never called an interval (C-R41).
2. **Name the driver.** Each range shows the driver that moves it most (for W1, reasoning tokens; for W2, output tokens; for W3, throughput per replica). Drivers are quantities from T0 or builder-set assumptions, never price noise.
3. **Round to the range.** Totals are rounded to two significant figures (builder-set) and never shown tighter than their low-to-high span; by analogy, JCGM 100:2008 7.2.6 says estimates "should not be given with an excessive number of digits" (C-U14). Unit prices keep their published precision on price rows only (C-R40).
4. **Say "estimate".** The UK Government Statistical Service guidance is to "convey a range of possible outcomes" and to use the word estimates throughout, on guidance marked "under review" (C-U15). All three hyperscaler calculators label their output as estimates (C-U7, C-U8, C-U6).
5. **Show the price date and the dated steps.** Each total carries "prices as of" with the oldest price date among its lines, and splits the period at any dated step (C-R10); the post-step price feeds the high case.
6. **Sum by case.** A combined total adds same-case values (low with low), which assumes the drivers move together and gives a wide, conservative range. This is labelled; a narrower combination needs a stated correlation.
7. **Flag what is not totalled.** Lines with no readable price (Datadog overage, L72; Lakera, whose pricing the #35 open questions list as not published on pages that render without scripts) appear as "not priced" rows next to the total, not as zero. Bedrock Provisioned Throughput has readable prices (L20) but no published model-unit throughput (C-P3), so it appears as "not sized".

Words for likelihood follow IPCC's calibrated scale only if a probability is given ("Likely 66-100% probability") (C-U16); this record gives none, so it uses no likelihood words. NIST's convention (report U with its coverage factor k) applies only to measured quantities (C-U17); no number here is measured.

## Currency and region policy

The issue's open question was the default currency and region. Proposal (Open questions asks maintainers to confirm):

- **Pricing currency: USD for every line.** Every price page and file read here lists USD (C-A5, C-A14, C-A2); the EUR and GBP figures in L75 are Azure Retail Prices API conversions requested on purpose. AWS bills in USD by default (C-F19); Azure Enterprise Agreement price sheets and Google and AWS local-currency billing are the exceptions (C-F10, C-F21, C-F19). Store the USD price string as published; never store a converted price as the source price (C-R17).
- **Display currency: the T0 budget currency.** Convert totals once, in the estimate layer, with a recorded rate, source and date. Default rate source: the ECB euro reference rates for EUR, GBP and JPY (2026-10-16: 1 EUR = 1.1253 USD), cited as the ECB requires ("the ECB must be cited as the source") (C-F20). Show the converted figure as indicative: providers bill at their own rates (Azure: London closing spot rates captured before month end, applied for the next month; Google: rates "set at the beginning of every month"; AWS: "determined when your invoice is created") (C-F10, C-F21, C-F19).
- **FX band.** Widen non-USD totals by the gap between the reference rate and the provider's rate. The gap computed here is the Azure Retail Prices API's implied EUR factor 0.880 (an estimate, not the invoice rate, C-F10) against the ECB's 0.889 (about 1 percent, estimated, L75); a builder-set band of ±2 percent covers it with margin. Surcharges (Google BRL +13.83 percent) are separate lines (L77).
- **Region: from the T0 residency field.** If residency is set, price the region and endpoint type it allows, and show the residency multiplier as its own line (L67). If residency is empty, price the provider's global or default endpoint and label the estimate "region not chosen"; show the cheapest compliant region as low and the most expensive candidate region as high. For region-bound SKUs with no global endpoint, the reference regions used in this record are us-east-1 (AWS), eastus or eastus2 (Azure) and the Global endpoint (Vertex), because those are the regions the price files were read for; they are a fallback, not a recommendation. When residency is empty, region is a price driver and, with the dated steps (C-R10), one of the two places where prices differ between cases.
- **Region deltas are not multipliers.** Azure Standard_D4s_v5 (Linux, Consumption) is USD 0.192 per hour in eastus and USD 0.23 per hour in westeurope; Azure Regional PTU is USD 2.0 per PTU-hour in eastus2 and USD 2.72 in westeurope (Azure Retail Prices API, read 2026-10-16; L76, L17). Look up the region's own price; do not scale a US price.
- **Tax: excluded and labelled.** Every total says "excluding tax"; the tax line is the user's, by billing address (C-R39, L78).
- **Credits and free tiers: separate lines.** Free allowances are applied once at their stated scope (C-R36); credits appear as a negative line with an expiry date, never netted into unit prices (L79).

## Compatibility proposals

**Source checked** means checked against primary documentation, not independently reproduced.

**Rule interpretation (candidate revision 0.2).** Each row has one fixed v1 severity (`error`, `warning` or `info`), separate from outcome (`pass`, `fail`, `unknown`, `skipped`). The condition describes the violation: known applicable violation is `fail`; known absence of that violation is `pass`; non-applicability is `skipped` with `not_applicable`; missing eligible inputs are `unknown` with `missing_evidence` and `on_unknown: review`. Resolve conflicts using #41's scope, version/date and authority order before taking the conservative equal-rank numeric value, flagged disputed. Only missing eligible evidence or genuine unresolved ties yield `unknown`; conflict alone does not. Hypotheses remain unaccepted and cannot drive production verdicts. Suffixed rows split the old condition; the unsuffixed ID is retained for its stated scope. No frozen schema or executable ruleset changes. See merged [T0 modes and evidence policy](../T0-configuration/deployment-mode.md), [#126 rule review](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/pull/126) and [#127 evidence review](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/pull/127); both PRs are merged partial decision records. ## Pricing and capacity

This record is the pricing and capacity method: Unit normalisation, Line-item catalog, Worked scenarios and Capacity vs usage above. Its prices are dated samples that prove the units and drive the scenarios. They are not pricebook proposals (see Structured artifacts).

## Recommendation and rationale

1. **Normalise, do not convert in place.** Keep every price as published (string, unit, quantity, currency, date) and derive canonical per-unit prices with the factor recorded. The unit problem (at least nine incompatible price units, catalogued as U1 to U30) and the unit-field conflicts (C-R2) make any other approach lossy.
2. **Build the range from quantities.** Low, expected and high come from T0 quantities and builder-set drivers (cache hit ratio, output and reasoning length, tokenizer ratio, utilisation, throughput), with prices fixed except at a dated step (C-R10) or an unset region. On the reference workload this already spans 4.3 times for API mode (W1).
3. **Show capacity with its utilisation and reserve status.** Show utilisation and break-even next to the usage alternative where those inputs are available, preserving unknowns otherwise. The current examples disclose **headroom not applied**; rounding and replica floors do not adopt a reserve. On the reference workload, peak-sized capacity runs at about a third of its throughput, so reserved PTUs cost about three times pay-as-you-go and hourly PTUs about 8.5 times (W2); a two-replica GPU floor runs at 2.6 to 27 percent (W3), and the same model on Workers AI is about one sixth of the self-hosted figure.
4. **Total the components.** The operations lines from #35 to #38 add 9 to 22 percent of an API build's total at this volume (estimated: add-ons / total, 22 / 18 / 8.8 percent in W4), about a fifth in the low and expected cases; judge tokens and guardrail checks matter, log ingestion does not.
5. **USD in, T0 currency out, tax excluded, dates always.** Convert once with a dated reference rate and an FX band; split periods at dated steps; refuse undated totals.
6. **Snapshot what #42 needs to re-price,** using stable ids, not names.
