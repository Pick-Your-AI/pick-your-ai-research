## User decision

Which signals must be watched once the build runs?

In scope: health, quality, latency, cost, usage, safety and data-freshness signals; alert thresholds; privacy of logged data. Out of scope: operating a monitoring stack.

Inputs. The Guardrails record ([#35](guardrails.md)), the Evaluation record ([#36](evaluation.md)) and the Deployment record ([#37](deployment.md)) sit in this folder and are cited here by id: #35 rule ids (`G-R21`) and control names (`trace-content-masking`), #36 rule ids (`E-R18`) and gate ids (`G1` to `G12`, with the meanings in the #36 gate table), #37 step, readiness, rule and evidence ids (`#37 S29`, `R35`, `D-R11`, `D-M13`). Rules those records own are cited, not restated. The #2 (T0) fields that set thresholds are latency target (percentile and value), throughput and concurrency, volume, data classification, residency and budget (amount, currency, period). The original pass did not hold #11, #16, #25 or #28. The separately dated [engine input integration](engine-input-integration.md) now reconciles the researched #11 releases with the engine signal names below; the other input reviews remain separate. Where a threshold needs a field no record defines yet, the row says so and Open questions names the owner.

### Placement rules used in this record

Mode follows the serving-component decision in [T0](../T0-configuration/deployment-mode.md), merged through #81: user-operated serving is `self_hosted`; provider-operated dedicated or reserved capacity is `managed`; provider-operated shared capacity is `api`. Billing units and customer-owned deployment objects do not determine mode. Hyperscaler on-demand/shared inference is `api`; dedicated endpoints and provisioned throughput are `managed`. Training runs do not set serving mode. Select the actual offering/capacity rather than inferring a mode from the provider name.

- A metric the platform emits about a deployment or account (Azure Monitor, CloudWatch, Cloud Monitoring) is placed in T2, even when the platform also serves API-mode traffic.
- A field returned on each response (headers, usage objects, stop reasons) is placed in T1 and applies wherever that response is received.

### Alert classes

Every signal carries one alert class. The class decides who is told and how fast; the threshold decides when.

| class | meaning | used for | basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| page | a person acts now | user-visible symptoms only: SLO burn rate on the latency and error SLIs, a spend stop that is rejecting requests, a serving pool with no ready endpoints | symptom-first alerting (M-O1, M-O2) |
| ticket | handled in working hours | causes and slow burns: saturation, pipeline failures, freshness lag, lifecycle dates, budget fractions | the 3-day burn-rate ticket in the SRE Workbook (M-O3) |
| dashboard | shown, not alerted | diagnostics that explain a page or ticket | M-O2 |
| gate | must return data before a rollout step continues | #37 S34 and R33 (alarms that exist and return data before an update); R24 (dashboards, a forced alert and a telemetry-failure alert exist); R26 (the promotion rule lists a quality metric with data for the canary cohort) | [#37](deployment.md) |
| audit | configuration state, checked at each deploy and on a schedule | content-capture flags, retention settings, sink regions, provider logging switches | this record (P1 to P12 below) |

### Four findings that shape the record

1. No opened source publishes a threshold for LLM quality, safety, refusal or cost alerts. Every threshold in this record is derived from a T0 target, published with its source, taken from the #36 baseline, or builder-set. The published numbers are platform limits, tool defaults and starting-point guidance: Azure PTU utilisation at or above 100 percent throttles (M-M1); llm-d alert defaults (M-S1); the Bedrock output-tokens-per-second alarm starting point of 80 percent of baseline (M-M11); Google budget defaults of 50, 90 and 100 percent (M-C4); LiteLLM `threshold_crossed` at 85 and 95 percent (M-C22); SRE Workbook burn rates of 14.4, 6 and 1 (M-O3); the AgentCore online-evaluation console sampling default of 10 percent (M-Q7); and Foundry's agent-dashboard investigation levels of 10 seconds and 95 percent (M-Q2). No number in this record is measured.
2. Billing data lags the spend it reports, and most budget alerts only notify. Published delays: Anthropic usage and cost "typically" within 5 minutes (M-A1); Azure cost data typically available within 8 to 24 hours for EA and MCA subscriptions and up to 72 hours for pay-as-you-go (M-A30), with budgets evaluated every 24 hours (M-C3); AWS Budgets updated up to three times a day (M-A2); Google budget notifications multiple times per day (M-C4). No opened page states the OpenAI usage or cost delay (M-H20). Provider-side stops exist at the OpenAI organization and project hard limits (M-A4, M-A28), the Anthropic tier cap and workspace limits (M-A5, M-C12), the Gemini API monthly spend caps (M-A31), Cloudflare spend limits (M-C25) and gateway budgets (M-C5, M-C27); AWS Budgets can run an IAM or SCP action (M-C17); Azure OpenAI has no hard limit (M-R6, M-C21). Real-time budget alerts are therefore computed in-process from tokens times price (SG56) and reconciled against the billing reports.
3. The OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions have no release. Every document in the `semantic-conventions-genai` repository is Status Development and the changelog shows only an Unreleased section (M-O4); a breaking change has already merged to main: the `gen_ai.client.token.usage` histogram is replaced by per-direction, per-operation histograms (`gen_ai.client.inference.operation.input_tokens` and `.output_tokens`), with new `gen_ai.client.inference.usage.*` counters added alongside (M-O5 and the 374 enhancement fragment, M-N301). #36 G12 asks for "a pinned convention version". With no release to pin, this record proposes to the #36 owner a reading of G12, not a new gate: pin the instrumentation package version, the `OTEL_SEMCONV_STABILITY_OPT_IN` value where the package has one (the deprecated `opentelemetry-instrumentation-openai-v2`, M-O18; its replacement uses the latest experimental conventions unconditionally, M-N311), the `semantic-conventions-genai` commit read here (`f3c6d0c9bb66`, committed 2026-10-14), and a rename map from builder metric names to `gen_ai.*` names (SG148, M-R36).
4. Content-capture defaults disagree across tools, so the same build can leak prompts through one exporter and not another. OpenTelemetry GenAI instrumentation captures no content by default (`NO_CONTENT`, M-O6); OpenInference hide flags default to False, so content is captured (M-O7); the OpenAI Agents SDK includes sensitive data by default (M-X1); Cloudflare AI Gateway logs are on by default and can include the prompt and response (M-M2); Anthropic's OpenTelemetry export defaults to metadata only (M-L1). The [OpenLLMetry source follow-up](#post-121-source-reconciliation-openllmetry-trace-content) adds its content-on default and selective workflow/context overrides (F01, checked 2026-10-18). The content-capture audit (SG147/P1) checks every exporter and effective override; a global flag alone does not establish what was exported.

## Compatibility proposals

**Source checked** means checked against primary documentation, not independently reproduced.

**Rule interpretation (candidate revision 0.2).** Each row has one fixed v1 severity (`error`, `warning` or `info`), separate from outcome (`pass`, `fail`, `unknown`, `skipped`). The condition describes the violation: known applicable violation is `fail`; known absence of that violation is `pass`; non-applicability is `skipped` with `not_applicable`; missing eligible inputs are `unknown` with `missing_evidence` and `on_unknown: review`. Resolve conflicts using #41's scope, version/date and authority order before taking the conservative equal-rank numeric value, flagged disputed. Only missing eligible evidence or genuine unresolved ties yield `unknown`; conflict alone does not. Hypotheses remain unaccepted and cannot drive production verdicts. Suffixed rows split the old condition; the unsuffixed ID is retained for its stated scope. No frozen schema or executable ruleset changes. See merged [T0 modes and evidence policy](../T0-configuration/deployment-mode.md), [#126 rule review](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/pull/126) and [#127 evidence review](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/pull/127); both PRs are merged partial decision records. Their scoped owner decisions apply, while open #40/#41 acceptance and next-version adoption remain separate.

Severity and outcome follow the interpretation above; missing eligible evidence or unresolved equal-rank ties require review. Participants that are not T0 fields today (retention, availability target, hard-stop flag, high-risk status) are named as such.

Rules owned by sibling records are cited, not restated. #35: G-R12 (an enforcing guardrail wired detect-only or fail-open), G-R20 (Gemini API Unpaid Services with personal data outside the EEA), G-R21 (Covered Models under zero retention), G-R22 (stateful provider features under zero retention), G-R23 (in-region processing with global routing), G-R24 (abuse-monitoring human review), G-R25 (vLLM and TGI usage telemetry), G-R26 (Ollama and llama.cpp network policy), G-R27 (trace content masking), G-R28 (CMEK on Vertex), G-R32 (non-Google models on Vertex under zero retention), G-R33 (CMEK and abuse-monitoring logs). #36: E-R14 (judge routing), E-R16 (region fixed at provisioning), E-R17 (exporter protocol), E-R18 (span drops), E-R19 (retention against trace-derived baselines), E-H5 (sample size). #37: D-R1 (moving aliases), D-R2 (retirement within build life), D-R10 (tier limits), D-R11 (Anthropic spend cap with retries on), D-R14 (Bedrock Legacy base and fine-tuning), D-R15 (Azure fine-tune retirement dates), D-R35 (SageMaker rollout alarms), D-R49 (Kubernetes startup-probe window shorter than the cold start), D-R80 (exporter not in a pipeline), D-R81 (no monitoring before traffic), D-R86 (OpenAI residency outside the US). ## Recommendation and rationale

Watch the minimum catalog for the build's mode, page only on symptoms, and audit the privacy state at every deploy.

1. Page on three things only: the multiwindow burn rate on the client-measured latency and error SLIs (SG157), a spend stop that is rejecting requests (SG4, SG5, SG65, SG68, SG63), and a serving pool with no ready endpoints (SG89). 5. Pin the telemetry conventions as finding 3 proposes for #36 G12, and smoke-test every metric name after an engine or instrumentation upgrade (M-R29, M-R37).

All three are mutable guides, not a pinned SDK/binary execution result. Thus an
OTel-disabled SDK deployment cannot be labeled telemetry-free from this evidence;

## Third-party tool throttling: GitHub and Slack — 2026-10-18

This bounded #26 → #38 supplement starts with two operation families already in
[T4's inventory](../T4-agents-integrations/tools-apis.md). It proposes monitoring
inputs, not a new universal retry policy or a change to the frozen integration
rate-limit field. Both sources were opened on 2026-10-18; they are mutable,
undated publisher documentation, source-checked / `unverified` for independent
runtime behavior. No API load test, private workspace read, credential change,
message send or rate-limit bypass was performed.

**GitHub REST (M-TR1).** [The primary guide](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)
identifies primary resource buckets with `x-ratelimit-resource`, limit/remaining/
used counts and reset time in UTC epoch seconds. Limits depend on authentication
and endpoint; the T4 personal-token example is not every installation's budget.
GitHub says response headers take precedence over the rate-limit endpoint and
may vary between responses, so remaining counts need not decrease monotonically.
Primary exhaustion gives 403/429 with remaining zero. Secondary throttling also
gives 403/429 but is identified by the error message; it need not exhaust the
primary bucket, and no secondary-budget status query exists. Follow a supplied
`retry-after` in seconds; with remaining zero, respect the reset timestamp;
otherwise the secondary-limit guidance says to wait at least one minute.
Repeated secondary failures require increasing backoff and a bounded retry count.
These are published values/conditions, not observed tenant limits. A generic 403
alone is insufficient evidence of throttling or permission to retry a denied call.

**Slack Web API (M-TR2).** [The primary guide](https://docs.slack.dev/apis/web-api/rate-limits/)
scopes limits by method, workspace and app. HTTP 429 carries `Retry-After` in
seconds; the same method/workspace restriction applies across that app's tokens.
Other methods or workspaces are not restricted by that particular response, which
does not certify that they have remaining capacity. Method tiers, pagination and
app/install cohort matter; preserve T4's dated B42/B43 history/replies cohort
readback rather than applying one blanket per-minute limit. Exact burst limits
are unpublished. A retry delay is not a rate, billable quantity or guaranteed
recovery time, and rotating tokens is not an acceptable substitute for waiting.

### Proposed observation fields and acceptance examples

| Observation | Type / unit | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provider, selected operation, resource/method bucket and scope binding | identifiers; no unit | #26 supplies operation and integration identity. Retain only an approved non-secret scope reference; never record token values or request/response content for this signal. |
| Throttle classification and missing evidence | enum-like proposed label | Distinguish documented primary/secondary/method throttle, permission failure and unknown; status alone may be insufficient. Keep original header/error classification evidence privately with appropriate minimization. |
