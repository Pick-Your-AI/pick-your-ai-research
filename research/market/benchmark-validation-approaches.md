# MR-09 background: approaches for validating benchmark comparisons

Purpose: one place that compares the usual ways to validate benchmark and harness comparisons, with cost and what each can and cannot settle. It is most relevant to the open #62 criterion "Pin models harnesses budgets and task versions": approaches 2 (fixed reference scaffold) and 10 (desk comparability checklist), and the reporting recommendations under approach 6, list what has to be pinned. It also gives a budget ladder for any future replication proposal.

Related: [benchmark-validation.md](benchmark-validation.md) already cites Miller 2024, Bjarnason et al. 2026 and Madaan et al.; the T6 [evaluation note](../T6-deployment-operations/evaluation.md) cites Terminal-Bench 2.0, FormatSpread and GSM1k. New here are the side-by-side comparison and AI Agents That Matter, HAL, the Agentic Benchmark Checklist and tinyBenchmarks.

A benchmark difference should count as real only when it survives four checks: the configuration was held fixed (a controlled harness grid or a fixed scaffold); the gap exceeds paired error bars over repeated runs; it shows up on full completion as well as the mean score; and the tasks pass a quality and contamination audit. The best value comes from cheap statistics and metric separation, which remove most false claims. Controlled harness grids are the most effective but cost thousands of dollars, and fresh private tasks are the strongest guard against contamination.

## The problem

A benchmark score belongs to a whole configuration, not to a model. The same model gets different results depending on the agent harness, the token and turn budget, the prompt, the task-set version and the grader. A difference between two published numbers means something only if those factors were held fixed or measured.

Harness-Bench shows the size of the effect. Its site reports harness gaps of 5.0 to 9.5 points for nine models and 39.2 points for one model, across 7 harnesses and 10 model backends (https://www.harness-bench.ai/). For one model, the mean score varies by 1.93 points (SD) across harnesses while the full-credit rate varies by 9.12 points.

Comparability usually breaks in five places: configuration drift (harness, prompt, sampling, budget, provider, tools); version drift (Harness-Bench's paper describes 106 tasks, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922, its site 300); noise (a single run on a few hundred tasks has a margin of error that often exceeds the claimed gap); contamination; and grader or task defects.

## Approaches compared

| # | Approach | Question it answers | Value | Best use | Effectiveness (evidence) | Evidence quality | Cost and quantity needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Controlled model × harness grid | How much does the harness change the score for each model? | High | Proving or measuring harness effects | High: Terminal-Bench 2.0 ran 6 agents × 16 models, 32,155 trials | Independent measurement | High: HAL spent about $40,000 on 21,730 rollouts |
| 2 | Fixed reference scaffold | Which model is better when the harness is held constant? | High | Ranking models fairly | High for model ranking; hides harness effects | Independent measurement | Medium: one scaffold, all models |
| 3 | Paired statistics and power analysis | Is the gap bigger than noise? | Very high | Every comparison | High: clustered SEs can be over 3× naive SEs | Peer-reviewed method | Very low: arithmetic on existing per-task results |
| 4 | Repeated trials and reliability metrics (pass^k) | Is the result stable across runs? | High | Agents, long tasks | High: single SWE-bench Verified runs vary 2.2 to 6.0 points | Peer-reviewed and workshop papers | Medium: 3 to 5+ runs per pair |
| 5 | Metric separation and cost frontiers | Does the mean hide incomplete delivery or high cost? | High | Delivery-style workloads, buying decisions | High: costs differ almost 100× at similar accuracy | Independent measurement | Very low if per-task scores and costs are logged |
| 6 | Sensitivity analysis (prompt, format, grader) | How fragile is the result to small changes? | Medium-high | Close calls, single-prompt claims | High: format spread up to 76 points | Peer-reviewed | Low: under $10 per task for one model |
| 7 | Contamination detection | Has the model seen the tasks? | High | Public, older benchmarks | Medium: shows likely contamination, cannot prove absence | Mixed | Low to high depending on method |
| 8 | Fresh, dated or private test sets | Do results hold on unseen tasks? | High | Final validation of a claim | High: GSM1k drops up to 13% | Independent measurement | High: new tasks written and kept private |
| 9 | Benchmark quality audit (ABC, BetterBench) | Are the tasks and graders valid? | Very high | Before trusting any benchmark | High: flaws misstate performance by up to 100% | Peer-reviewed | Medium: manual review per benchmark |
| 10 | Desk comparability audit of published claims | Can two published numbers be compared at all? | High | No compute budget | Medium: filters bad comparisons, measures nothing | Primary documentation | Very low: metadata checklist |
| 11 | Trace and failure analysis | Why do configurations differ? | Medium-high | Explaining harness effects | Medium: 51% of failures judged spurious in one study | Independent measurement | Medium: needs logs and review time |
| 12 | Efficient subsampling (IRT, tinyBenchmarks) | Can we estimate scores with fewer tasks? | Medium | Cheap screening | Mixed: under 2% error on means, about 12% of pairwise rankings can flip | Peer-reviewed, contested | Very low: about 100 items per scenario |

## Approach details (initial source access 2026-10-16; Miller v1 recheck 2026-10-25)

1. Controlled model × harness grid. Terminal-Bench 2.0 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11868): 6 agents × 16 models, 89 tasks, at least 5 runs per pair, 32,155 trials; Gemini 2.5 Pro gained 17% with one agent over another; authors conclude model choice usually matters more than scaffold. Harness-Bench site: 7 harnesses × 10 models, 20,454 runs, 200K-token context and 150-turn limits. HAL (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977v1): 21,730 rollouts, 9 models, 9 benchmarks, about $40,000; task-specific scaffolds beat a generalist in 11 of 12 runs on SWE-bench Verified Mini; providers can change weights or quantization behind the same endpoint.
2. Fixed reference scaffold. SWE-bench Bash Only (https://swebench.com/bash-only.html): same mini-SWE-agent loop at temperature 0 for all models. Epoch AI (https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified): fixed scaffold, 2M uncached-token cap; its 2026-02-12 scaffold upgrade raised scores "significantly", so pre/post results are not comparable. METR and SWE-bench Pro (50-turn cap) use the same pattern.
3. Paired statistics and power analysis. Miller, Anthropic 2024 (https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1; §§2.1–3.1, 5 and Tables 2, 4; rechecked 2026-10-25): CLT SEs, clustered SEs (up to 3.05× naive on DROP), resampling and paired differences. Table 2's HumanEval SE of 3.2% for 164 problems uses fictional models and numbers, not an empirical benchmark result. Section 5's approximately 969 independent questions is an illustrative paired power calculation for a 3-percentage-point gap, 80% power, 5% Type I error, zero conditional answer variances and question-level difference variance 1/9; it is not a requirement derived from question count alone.
4. Repeated trials. Bjarnason et al. 2026 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150v1): 60,000 SWE-bench Verified trajectories; single-run pass@1 shifts 2.2 to 6.0 points; std over 1.5 points even at temperature 0; recommend pass@k and pass^k with k>1. τ-bench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) pass^k: GPT-4o retail pass^1 0.604 vs pass^4 0.383. Inspect AI supports epochs with pass@k and pass^k reducers.
5. Metric separation and cost. Harness-Bench: mean, R0.8, full credit; per-model harness choice raised full credit 2.53 to 2.73 points while the mean moved ±0.14. AI Agents That Matter (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502v1): LATS $134.50 for 88.0% vs simple retry $2.51 for 92.0% on HumanEval. HAL: most expensive model on the cost-accuracy frontier in only 1 of 9 benchmarks.
6. Sensitivity. FormatSpread (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324v1): up to 76 points of spread from formatting on LLaMA-2-13B, median 6.4 on GPT-3.5, under $10 per task. Biderman et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782v1): scoring style moved Mistral-7B ARC-Challenge from 50.1 to 72.4 and flipped rankings; recommends sharing exact prompts, code with commit IDs, model outputs, and versioned tasks.
7. Contamination. GPT-4 report substring check (73% of AP US History items contaminated) needs the training corpus. Canary strings: GPT-4-base reproduced the BIG-bench canary. Min-K% Prob (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16789v3) needs logprobs. None proves absence.
8. Fresh/dated/private sets. GSM1k (https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00332v1): 1,250 new problems, drops up to 13%. LiveCodeBench (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07974v2): Codestral 36.5 → 28.3 pass@1 after cutoff. ARC Prize semi-private sets; SWE-bench Pro held-out and commercial sets. Private results cannot be reproduced by outsiders.
9. Quality audit. Agentic Benchmark Checklist (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825v5): 42 items; of 10 audited agent benchmarks 7 had task-validity flaws, all 10 reporting gaps; τ-bench empty responses scored 38%. SWE-bench Verified filtered 68.3% of 1,699 screened tasks. SWE-Bench+ (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06992v2): 32.67% of passing patches had leaked solutions. BetterBench (https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12990v1): 14 of 24 benchmarks reported no uncertainty.
10. Desk comparability audit. Score each published claim against a metadata checklist: task-set version, model version, provider, harness and version, budgets, sampling, runs, metric definition, uncertainty, date, source. Cheapest; measures nothing new.
11. Trace analysis. HAL: instruction violations in over 60% of failures on two benchmarks; 8 cases of agents finding answers online. METR GPT-4o report: 51% of 152 failures judged spurious.
12. Subsampling. tinyBenchmarks (https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2402.14992): about 100 items, under 2% error. Madaan et al. (https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2406.10229): such subsets can flip about 12% of pairwise rankings.

## Combining approaches by budget

| Tier | Budget | Approaches | Can conclude | Cannot conclude |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Desk only | No compute | 10, 9, 3 recomputed from published per-task data, 7 from public evidence | Which published comparisons are invalid or underpowered; which sources conflict | Whether any harness effect is real |
| 1 Small replication | Tens to hundreds of dollars | + 2 on a task subset, 4 with 3 to 5 runs, 5, 6 | Whether a published gap reproduces for 1 or 2 pairs, with error bars | General harness effects across models |
| 2 Controlled grid | Thousands of dollars | + 1 with fixed budgets, 11, cost frontiers | Which harness differences survive, per model, with cost | Results on unseen tasks |
| 3 Fresh tasks | Expert time plus the grid | + 8 | Whether surviving effects hold without contamination | Universal rankings |

## Limits

Sources disagree on whether model or harness matters more (Terminal-Bench 2.0 vs Harness-Bench's 39.2-point gap). Subsampling is contested. Standard errors cover the random variation represented by the estimator and its sampling/dependence model. Miller v1 §§2.1–3.1 includes question heterogeneity and stochastic answer variation, with repeated answers aggregated to question-level means; standard errors do not by themselves account for systematic configuration drift, grading bias or contamination. The accepted [protocol](benchmark-validation.md) still governs task/run/dependence-aware paired inference and owner-set margins; adoption of this background remains for appletechie/PUSHINGSQUARES to accept, change or decline. Private sets resist contamination but cannot be checked by outsiders. Harness-Bench arXiv v2 (revision 2026-10-15) is recorded in [benchmark-validation.md](benchmark-validation.md). Not opened: arXiv 2609.17394, 2606.08529, 2606.20683.
