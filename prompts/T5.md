### Architectural requirements

When T5 is enabled, the builder should be able to determine:

- Whether the model can be customized.
- Which fine-tuning methods and providers are permitted.
- Which frameworks support the selected model.
- What compute resources may be required.

`research/T5-training-fine-tuning/training-framework.md`

## Research objective

Determine whether a chosen model can be customized and which frameworks, training methods, services, and configurations are compatible with it.

Evaluate both self-hosted training frameworks and managed fine-tuning services.

## Framework candidates

Investigate current relevant solutions, including:

- Hugging Face Transformers Trainer.
- Hugging Face TRL.
- Hugging Face PEFT.
- Axolotl.
- Unsloth.
- LLaMA-Factory.
- torchtune.
- NVIDIA NeMo.
- PyTorch-based training workflows.
- Applicable cloud and managed fine-tuning offerings.
- Other relevant open-source or commercial frameworks.

These are research candidates, not verified compatibility claims or predetermined recommendations.

Verify current versions, supported models, available training methods, license terms, and documentation.

## Training methods

Research applicability and limitations of:

- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- Full-parameter fine-tuning.
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT).
- LoRA.
- QLoRA.
- Preference optimization, including DPO where supported.
- Adapter-based methods.
- Other documented training or post-training methods.

Distinguish fine-tuning from pretraining, model distillation, retrieval augmentation, and prompt engineering.

These are different activities and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Required comparison fields

Research:

- Framework or service ID.
- Version and release.
- License.
- Supported model architectures.
- Supported model versions.
- Required weight/model access.
- Supported training methods.
- Precision and quantization support.
- Distributed-training capabilities.
- Input dataset formats.
- Output artifact formats.
- Adapter and checkpoint handling.
- Export and portability.
- Managed versus self-hosted availability.
- Integration with training compute.
- Hardware and software prerequisites.
- Documentation and source provenance.

## Model access and licensing

Determine:

- Whether model weights are accessible.
- Whether the model license permits the desired training activity.
- Whether commercial fine-tuning is allowed.
- Whether redistribution of derived weights or adapters is restricted.
- Whether a provider offers managed fine-tuning.
- Whether fine-tuned artifacts are portable or provider-locked.
- Whether a particular method is supported for a given model version.

Do not assume open-weight availability automatically grants unrestricted training or redistribution rights.

## Acceptance criteria

Issue #30 requires:

- A verified framework/service candidate matrix.
- Supported training methods mapped to model access requirements.
- Evidence-backed licensing and permission research.
- Compatible input/output formats.
- Method/framework constraints in the #40 rule format.
- Clear differentiation between known and unknown support.
- Reviewed and merged decision record.
- Confirmed handoffs to #31, #32, and #33.

# 7. Track B — Issue #31: Training Compute

**Issue:** https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/issues/31

**Canonical kind:** `training_compute`

**Required decision record:**

`research/T5-training-fine-tuning/training-compute.md`

## Research objective

Define defensible methods for estimating the compute, memory, storage, time, and financial requirements of training and fine-tuning workloads.

Research must account for differences between training methods and execution environments.

## Required compute dimensions

- Base model architecture and size.
- Trainable parameter count.
- Frozen parameter count.
- Training method.
- Optimizer and optimizer-state memory.
- Gradient memory.
- Activation memory.
- Precision.
- Quantization.
- Batch size.
- Gradient accumulation.
- Sequence length.
- Gradient checkpointing.
- Adapter rank and configuration.
- GPU memory.
- Host RAM.
- GPU interconnect.
- Multi-GPU parallelism.
- CPU and storage requirements.
- Dataset size.
- Training tokens.
- Training steps and epochs.
- Checkpointing requirements.
- Estimated training duration.
- Throughput and utilization assumptions.

## Compute environments

Compare:

- Local workstation training.
- Single-GPU cloud instances.
- Multi-GPU cloud instances.
- Dedicated GPU servers.
- Managed training services.
- Distributed training environments.

Differentiate model memory requirements from total training memory requirements.

Do not assume inference VRAM calculators can directly determine training capacity.

Do not assume GPU VRAM simply adds together as unified memory across multiple devices.

## Estimation methodology

Develop explicitly stated formulas or reproducible procedures for estimating:

1. Base model and trainable parameter memory.
2. Optimizer and gradient memory.
3. Activation memory.
4. Other runtime overhead.
5. Dataset and checkpoint storage.
6. Training duration based on workload and observed throughput.
7. GPU usage costs.
8. Storage and data transfer costs.
9. Managed-service costs.
10. Total cost with uncertainty.

Where calculations require uncertain factors, express those assumptions and bounds.

Distinguish:

- Vendor-published figures.
- Independently measured figures.
- Derived estimates.
- Unverified hypotheses.

Use appropriate reference scenarios to demonstrate the methodology without inventing experimental results.

## Existing boundaries

T2 #10 owns the canonical GPU/accelerator catalog and inference sizing methodology.

T5 #31 owns training-specific compute estimation.

T2 #9 owns hosting and infrastructure offerings.

T6 #39 owns the consolidated cost model.

Reuse existing accelerator and hosting records rather than creating competing catalogs.

## Acceptance criteria

Issue #31 requires:

- Documented memory/capacity estimation methodology.
- Explicit uncertainty and assumptions.
- Representative sourced training scenarios.
- Coverage of managed and self-hosted approaches.
- Normalized pricing units and price provenance.
- Compute compatibility considerations.
- Confirmed handoff to #39.
- Reviewed and merged decision record.
